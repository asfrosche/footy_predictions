How the models are evaluated

Walk-forward, out of sample

The only out-of-sample result so far is a results-only Poisson benchmark, fitted walk-forward over 1,120 matches (2023/24 to 2026/27). Its log loss is 1.016 against 1.076 for the league average. The difference is -0.060 (95% interval −0.087 to −0.033).

Limits of these results

  • The walk-forward benchmark is the only out-of-sample result in the repository. It is a results-only model, not the six production versions.
  • The six production versions have not yet been tested out of sample against bookmaker odds.
  • The bookmaker comparison in the model review is in-sample (100 matches the ratings already contain), so it cannot be compared with the walk-forward figures.
  • Simulation counts of 100 per version are too small to separate close versions reliably. This has not yet been quantified.

Log loss and the Brier score are proper scoring rules. The Rank Probability Score is also reported, with its disputed status noted in the references below.

Odds and margin

Margin removed

Bookmaker prices include a margin, so their implied probabilities add up to more than 100%. The site removes that margin to show the probabilities. Odds are shown as bookmaker prices; the probabilities are a derived view, not a betting recommendation.

Closing odds are the market benchmark used in the papers below (Dixon & Coles, Koopman & Lit, Hvattum & Arntzen). Several of those papers report betting profits. Profit from a strategy is not evidence of a better probability model.

Papers

Citations checked against the publisher or repository record

Status shows what was confirmed. Confirmed means the citation and main claim match the record. Partly confirmed means the citation is right but at least one claim is not supported or is disputed. Preprint means the paper is not peer-reviewed. Full texts were not all read, so numbers quoted from abstracts should be checked before reuse.

Confirmed1997

Modelling association football scores and inefficiencies in the football betting market

M. J. Dixon & S. G. Coles · Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series C, 46(2), 265–280

Finding. A Poisson-based model fitted to English league and cup results from 1992–95. A betting strategy built on it returned a profit against 1995–96 odds.

Use here. The standard Poisson refinement: a low-score correction and time weighting for recent form.

The low-score correction and time weighting are from secondary summaries, not from the abstract.

Confirmed1982

Modelling association football scores

M. J. Maher · Statistica Neerlandica, 36(3), 109–118

Finding. An independent Poisson model describes football scores reasonably well. A bivariate Poisson with correlation of about 0.2 improves the fit.

Use here. The root of the results-only Poisson baseline in this project.

Confirmed2003

Analysis of sports data by using bivariate Poisson models

D. Karlis & I. Ntzoufras · Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series D, 52(3), 381–393

Finding. A shared third Poisson component links the two teams' goal counts. With no shared component the model reduces to independent Poisson.

Use here. A test for draw under-prediction in the current models.

The claim that Poisson models under-predict draws comes from secondary sources and has not been checked against the paper.

Partly confirmed2010

Using ELO ratings for match result prediction in association football

L. M. Hvattum & H. Arntzen · International Journal of Forecasting, 26(3), 460–470

Finding. Elo ratings are used as covariates in an ordered logit model and compared against six benchmark methods. Bookmaker-based methods were the strongest comparators.

Use here. A results-only benchmark that needs no squad data.

A later paper reports that the authors found Elo-based forecasts clearly inferior to bookmaker odds. The dataset used is not confirmed.

Confirmed2010

Bayesian hierarchical model for the prediction of football results

G. Baio & M. Blangiardo · Journal of Applied Statistics, 37(2), 253–264

Finding. A Bayesian hierarchical goal model with team strengths drawn from a common distribution. It identifies overshrinkage of the strongest and weakest teams, which a mixture model corrects. Tested on Serie A 1991–92 and 2007–08.

Use here. A warning about shrinkage in data-poor periods such as the start of a season.

Confirmed2015

A dynamic bivariate Poisson model for analysing and forecasting match results in the English Premier League

S. J. Koopman & R. Lit · Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series A, 178(1), 167–186

Finding. Team scoring intensities change over time within a bivariate Poisson. An out-of-sample test on the 2010–11 and 2011–12 Premier League seasons reported a positive return against bookmaker odds.

Use here. The closest published design to this project's walk-forward evaluation.

A 2019 follow-up (International Journal of Forecasting, 35(2), 797–809) found that the most accurate forecasts came from a different specification than the most profitable one.

Partly confirmed2012

pi-football: A Bayesian network model for forecasting association football match outcomes

A. C. Constantinou, N. E. Fenton & M. Neil · Knowledge-Based Systems, 36, 322–339

Finding. A Bayesian network that combines historical results with subjective expert variables. The authors forecast the 2010/11 Premier League.

Use here. The only published route into non-data inputs such as injuries and motivation.

The authors claim the model could beat bookmaker odds. That claim is not independently replicated in the sources checked.

Confirmed2012

Solving the problem of inadequate scoring rules for assessing probabilistic football forecast models

A. C. Constantinou & N. E. Fenton · Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports, 8(1)

Finding. Argues that the Rank Probability Score suits ordered outcomes (home, draw, away) better than the scoring rules used in earlier work.

Use here. Why this project reports the Rank Probability Score alongside log loss and the Brier score.

The recommendation is disputed. Wheatcroft (2019, arXiv 1908.08980) argues that the ignorance score does better in simulation.

Confirmed2017

A bivariate Weibull count model for forecasting association football scores

G. Boshnakov, T. Kharrat & I. G. McHale · International Journal of Forecasting, 33(2), 458–466

Finding. Goals are modelled as a Weibull count process, with a copula linking the two teams' counts.

Use here. Shows the Poisson assumption is a choice. A fit test is needed before adopting any alternative.

The claim of a better fit than Poisson, especially for away goals, was not confirmed in the sources checked.

Partly confirmed2019

A hybrid random forest to predict soccer matches in international tournaments

A. Groll, C. Ley, G. Schauberger & H. Van Eetvelde · Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports, 15(4), 271–287

Finding. A random forest on team covariates, with Poisson-estimated team strengths added as a covariate. Tested on all matches from the 2002–2014 World Cups, which is an out-of-sample split by tournament.

Use here. Shows that results-based strength combined with covariates is a serious design.

The reported beating of betting odds comes from the 2018 World Cup forecast, not from the 2002–2014 test.

Confirmed2021

A goal scoring probability model for shots based on synchronized positional and event data in football (soccer)

G. Anzer & P. Bauer · Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, 3, 624475

Finding. Shot-level goal probability from positional and event data for 105,627 Bundesliga shots. The best model is gradient-boosted trees with a reported RPS of 0.197.

Use here. The shot-level xG benchmark to compare any xG source against.

Preprint2020

Dynamic Bayesian forecasting of English Premier League match results with the Skellam distribution

R. C. Smit, F. Ravazzolo & L. Rossini · Bozen Economics & Management Paper Series, No. 72/2020 (working paper)

Finding. A dynamic Bayesian model of the goal difference using the Skellam distribution, with team scoring abilities that change over the season.

Use here. Models the goal difference directly, which is a different route from the Poisson pair.

A working paper, not peer-reviewed as far as the sources show. The 2018–19 validation was not checked.

Partly confirmed2021

A Bayesian inference approach for determining player abilities in football

G. A. Whitaker, R. Silva, D. Edwards & I. Kosmidis · Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series C, 70(1), 174–201

Finding. Player abilities are estimated with a Poisson model and feed a team scoring-rate model. Abilities from the 2013/14 Premier League season are used to predict over or under 2.5 goals.

Use here. The player-to-team link that would make squad data useful.

The season the over/under test covers was not confirmed.

Confirmed2023

Expected goals in football: Improving model performance and demonstrating value

J. Mead, A. O'Hare & P. McMenemy · PLOS ONE, 18(4), e0282295

Finding. Machine-learning expected goals with new features. The authors report that xG predicts future team success better than traditional statistics.

Use here. Supports the case for xG as an input once shot data is available.

Confirmed2023

Football results prediction and machine learning techniques

V. Chang, K. Hall & L. M. T. Doan · International Journal of Business and Systems Research, 17(5), 565–586

Finding. Machine-learning models on English Premier League matches, using expected goals as the base input and benchmarked against bookmaker models and chance. A Poisson-based classifier reached 52.3% accuracy on the 2020–21 season.

Use here. A machine-learning comparison that includes a market benchmark.

The full text is paywalled. The evaluation method was not confirmed as walk-forward.

Confirmed2025

Extending the Dixon and Coles model: an application to women's football data

R. Michels, M. Ötting & D. Karlis · arXiv 2307.02139 (2023); Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series C, 74(1), 2025

Finding. Shows the Dixon-Coles model is a special case of the Sarmanov family of bivariate distributions, which allows other marginals and dependence structures. Applied to women's league data.

Use here. A documented replacement for the ad hoc Dixon-Coles correction, to test on our own draw rate.

Preprint2021

On the dependence in football match outcomes: traditional model assumptions and an alternative proposal

M. Petretta, L. Schiavon & J. Diquigiovanni · arXiv 2103.07272; later in Statistical Modelling

Finding. Questions the Poisson marginals and the dependence structure behind Dixon-Coles, and proposes the Mar-Co model, which relaxes both.

Use here. An alternative to the draw correction to test as an ablation.

The year of the journal version is disputed in the sources (2024 or 2025).

Preprint2025

Bayesian weighted discrete-time dynamic models for association football prediction

R. Macrì-Demartino, L. Egidi & N. Torelli · arXiv 2508.05891 (2025)

Finding. Lets team attack and defence strengths change over time with commensurate priors. The authors report better predictive performance than other discrete-time dynamic models, and a shorter computation time.

Use here. The time-varying strength idea, in a Bayesian form.

A journal listing gives JRSS-C 2026. That was not confirmed.

Confirmed2025

Assessing machine learning and data imputation approaches to handle the issue of data sparsity in sports forecasting

F. Wunderlich et al. · Machine Learning (2025), doi:10.1007/S10994-024-06651-7

Finding. Hybrid and deep-learning models tested on more than 300,000 soccer matches. Imputation is assessed as a way to handle missing data.

Use here. A direct treatment of the sparse player-attribute problem this project has.
